Two Halves of Your Grade

Your grade comes from two different places, and they're set up to work differently on purpose.

🎯 The Standards Track

Calculus I breaks down into specific skills — derivative rules, limit techniques, integration, and more. Each one gets its own short, direct quiz, and the practice pages on this site are built to look just like it, so there are no surprises. Pass a standard once and it's yours for good. Don't pass it the first time? You'll get more chances at every midterm and during office hours. This is the mostly self-paced, skill-building side of the course.

🧩 Class Time, Capstones & Your Percentage

This is where the deeper, more open-ended work happens — the kind of problem you can't fully prep for with a formula sheet. In class you'll dig into challenging problems, explore ideas, and work on projects that stretch how you use the skills you're building. That work builds toward four capstone exams, where you show you can combine skills and reason through harder problems. Capstones plus your daily participation make up your course percentage.

A Day in This Class

Most days, class time looks like one of these three things:

📝 Standard-style practice. Solving problems similar to what you'll see on the standard quizzes — building the muscle memory you need to pass them.
🔍 Challenging, exploratory problems. Digging into something open-ended that goes beyond a single formula.
🛠️ Project work. Tying several skills together on something bigger than a single problem.

All of this counts toward your class participation grade — so showing up and engaging matters. It's also simply where you build the skills you'll need for the standards and capstones, so attendance pays off twice.

Midterms, Capstones & the Final

Throughout the semester, you'll take four capstone exams — one for each major part of the course — plus a standalone final exam at the end of the semester. All of these are graded like traditional exams and go straight into your course percentage; they're not part of the standards retake system.

The final exam is separate from the four capstones — it's its own exam at the end of the semester, on top of the four unit capstones.

💡 A Note on Extra Credit

Short version: you probably won't need it. Since you can always go back and retake a standard you haven't passed yet, there's a built-in way to raise your grade all semester long — no extra-credit assignment required.

That said, there are a few optional topics that go beyond the core of the course — things like related rates and harder L'Hôpital's Rule problems. If you want to go deeper, we'll post short video modules so you can learn them on your own. Pass three or more of these optional topics, and the course percentage you need for an A drops from 90% to 85% — a nice bonus if you're up for the extra challenge, not a requirement for anyone.
