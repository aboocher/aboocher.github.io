D5: Trig, Inverse Trig, Exponential & Log Derivatives

25 practice problems on the derivative rules for trigonometric, inverse trigonometric, exponential, and logarithmic functions — plus tangent lines that use them. Every argument here is plain $x$ (no chain rule needed yet).

  • Memorize the six trig derivatives cold: $\frac{d}{dx}\sin x=\cos x$, $\frac{d}{dx}\cos x=-\sin x$, $\frac{d}{dx}\tan x=\sec^2 x$, $\frac{d}{dx}\cot x=-\csc^2 x$, $\frac{d}{dx}\sec x=\sec x\tan x$, $\frac{d}{dx}\csc x=-\csc x\cot x$.
  • Inverse trig derivatives: $\frac{d}{dx}\arcsin x=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}$, $\frac{d}{dx}\arccos x=-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}$ (same denominator, opposite sign), and $\frac{d}{dx}\arctan x=\dfrac{1}{1+x^2}$.
  • $\frac{d}{dx}e^x=e^x$ — the one function that's its own derivative. For any other base, an extra $\ln(\text{base})$ tags along: $\frac{d}{dx}a^x=a^x\ln a$.
  • $\frac{d}{dx}\ln x=\dfrac{1}{x}$. For any other base, divide by $\ln(\text{base})$: $\frac{d}{dx}\log_a x=\dfrac{1}{x\ln a}$.
  • A coefficient in front of a term just carries through unchanged — differentiate the function, then multiply by the constant.
  • Tangent lines work exactly as before: find the point with $f(a)$, the slope with $f'(a)$, then write $y-f(a)=f'(a)(x-a)$.
