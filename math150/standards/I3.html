I3: Riemann Sums (Rectangles)

12 practice problems approximating the area under a curve with rectangles, using left, right, and midpoint Riemann sums. Give exact values, simplified as much as possible.

  • Split $[a,b]$ into $n$ equal subintervals, each of width $\Delta x=\dfrac{b-a}{n}$.
  • Left sum: the height of each rectangle is $f$ evaluated at the left edge of its subinterval. Right sum: evaluate at the right edge. Midpoint sum: evaluate at the midpoint of the subinterval.
  • Every Riemann sum has the same shape: $\text{Sum}=\Delta x\big[f(x_1)+f(x_2)+\cdots+f(x_n)\big]$ — one $\Delta x$ factored out front, times the sum of the $n$ heights.
  • List out the $n$ sample points first before evaluating $f$ at each one — that's where an off-by-one slip is most likely to happen.
  • For a midpoint sum, the sample points are $\Delta x$ apart just like left/right, but shifted by $\frac{\Delta x}{2}$ from the left endpoint of the interval.

Left Riemann Sums

1.

$\Delta x=\dfrac{4-0}{4}=1$. Left endpoints: $0,1,2,3$.

$f(0)=1,\;f(1)=2,\;f(2)=5,\;f(3)=10$

$\text{Sum}=1(1+2+5+10)=18$
