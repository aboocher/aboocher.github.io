L4: Evaluating Limits Algebraically (Level 2)

22 practice problems on two Level 2 algebraic limit techniques: simplifying a complex fraction before you can cancel, and multiplying by the conjugate to clear a square root.

  • Both families still start with direct substitution giving $\frac{0}{0}$ — that's still your signal to do more algebra, not your final answer.
  • Complex fractions: you can't factor or cancel until the numerator is a single fraction. Combine the two fractions on top with a common denominator first, then the whole expression is "divide by $(x-a)$," which is the same as multiplying by $\frac{1}{x-a}$.
  • Watch subtraction order in complex fractions: $\frac{1}{A}-\frac{1}{B}$ is the negative of $\frac{1}{B}-\frac{1}{A}$. Swapping the order in the original problem flips the sign of the final answer — nothing else changes.
  • Conjugates: when the $\frac{0}{0}$ comes from a square root, multiply numerator and denominator by the conjugate (same two terms, opposite sign in the middle). $(\sqrt{u}-k)(\sqrt{u}+k)=u-k^2$ clears the radical and produces a factor of $(x-a)$ you can cancel.
  • If the radical ends up on the bottom instead of the top after multiplying by the conjugate, that's expected — finish by canceling $(x-a)$ and substituting, same as always.
  • Simplify as far as possible before plugging in $x=a$ — that's what makes the $\frac{0}{0}$ turn into a real number.

Solutions

Complex Fractions — Linear Denominators

1.

Direct substitution gives $\frac{0}{0}$. Combine the top over a common denominator first.

$\displaystyle\frac{1}{x+2}-\frac{1}{2}=\frac{2-(x+2)}{2(x+2)}=\frac{-x}{2(x+2)}$

Dividing by $x$ (the outer denominator) means multiplying by $\frac{1}{x}$, and the $x$'s cancel:

$\displaystyle\frac{-x}{2(x+2)}\cdot\frac{1}{x}=\frac{-1}{2(x+2)}$

$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{\frac{1}{x+2}-\frac{1}{2}}{x}=\frac{-1}{2(2)}=-\frac{1}{4}$

↑ problem
2.

$\displaystyle\frac{1}{x+2}-\frac{1}{3}=\frac{3-(x+2)}{3(x+2)}=\frac{1-x}{3(x+2)}=\frac{-(x-1)}{3(x+2)}$

Dividing by $(x-1)$ cancels the common factor:

$\displaystyle\frac{-(x-1)}{3(x+2)(x-1)}=\frac{-1}{3(x+2)}$

$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 1}\frac{\frac{1}{x+2}-\frac{1}{3}}{x-1}=\frac{-1}{3(3)}=-\frac{1}{9}$

↑ problem
3.

Note the subtraction order is flipped from Problem 1's pattern — expect the opposite sign.

$\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{x+5}=\frac{(x+5)-4}{4(x+5)}=\frac{x+1}{4(x+5)}$

Dividing by $(x+1)$ cancels:

$\displaystyle\frac{x+1}{4(x+5)(x+1)}=\frac{1}{4(x+5)}$

$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to -1}\frac{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{x+5}}{x+1}=\frac{1}{4(4)}=\frac{1}{16}$

↑ problem
4.

$\displaystyle\frac{1}{x+3}-\frac{1}{5}=\frac{5-(x+3)}{5(x+3)}=\frac{2-x}{5(x+3)}=\frac{-(x-2)}{5(x+3)}$

Dividing by $(x-2)$ cancels:

$\displaystyle\frac{-(x-2)}{5(x+3)(x-2)}=\frac{-1}{5(x+3)}$

$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 2}\frac{\frac{1}{x+3}-\frac{1}{5}}{x-2}=\frac{-1}{5(5)}=-\frac{1}{25}$

↑ problem
5.

Flipped order again, like Problem 3 — the sign comes out positive.

$\displaystyle\frac{1}{6}-\frac{1}{x+9}=\frac{(x+9)-6}{6(x+9)}=\frac{x+3}{6(x+9)}$

Dividing by $(x+3)$ cancels:

$\displaystyle\frac{x+3}{6(x+9)(x+3)}=\frac{1}{6(x+9)}$

$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to -3}\frac{\frac{1}{6}-\frac{1}{x+9}}{x+3}=\frac{1}{6(6)}=\frac{1}{36}$

↑ problem
6.

$\displaystyle\frac{1}{x+9}-\frac{1}{7}=\frac{7-(x+9)}{7(x+9)}=\frac{-x-2}{7(x+9)}=\frac{-(x+2)}{7(x+9)}$

Dividing by $(x+2)$ cancels:

$\displaystyle\frac{-(x+2)}{7(x+9)(x+2)}=\frac{-1}{7(x+9)}$

$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to -2}\frac{\frac{1}{x+9}-\frac{1}{7}}{x+2}=\frac{-1}{7(7)}=-\frac{1}{49}$

↑ problem

Complex Fractions — Squared Denominators

7.

Combine over a common denominator, then factor the numerator as a difference of squares.

$\displaystyle\frac{1}{x^2}-\frac{1}{4}=\frac{4-x^2}{4x^2}=\frac{-(x-2)(x+2)}{4x^2}$

Dividing by $(x-2)$ cancels that factor, leaving an $(x+2)$ behind:

$\displaystyle\frac{-(x-2)(x+2)}{4x^2(x-2)}=\frac{-(x+2)}{4x^2}$

$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 2}\frac{\frac{1}{x^2}-\frac{1}{4}}{x-2}=\frac{-(2+2)}{4(4)}=\frac{-4}{16}=-\frac{1}{4}$

↑ problem
8.

$\displaystyle\frac{1}{x^2}-\frac{1}{9}=\frac{9-x^2}{9x^2}=\frac{-(x-3)(x+3)}{9x^2}$

Dividing by $(x+3)$ cancels that factor:

$\displaystyle\frac{-(x-3)(x+3)}{9x^2(x+3)}=\frac{-(x-3)}{9x^2}$

$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to -3}\frac{\frac{1}{x^2}-\frac{1}{9}}{x+3}=\frac{-(-3-3)}{9(9)}=\frac{6}{81}=\frac{2}{27}$

↑ problem
9.

Treat $(x+1)$ as the variable being squared; the same difference-of-squares pattern applies.

$\displaystyle\frac{1}{(x+1)^2}-\frac{1}{9}=\frac{9-(x+1)^2}{9(x+1)^2}=\frac{-\big[(x+1)-3\big]\big[(x+1)+3\big]}{9(x+1)^2}=\frac{-(x-2)(x+4)}{9(x+1)^2}$

Dividing by $(x-2)$ cancels:

$\displaystyle\frac{-(x-2)(x+4)}{9(x+1)^2(x-2)}=\frac{-(x+4)}{9(x+1)^2}$

$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 2}\frac{\frac{1}{(x+1)^2}-\frac{1}{9}}{x-2}=\frac{-(2+4)}{9(3)^2}=\frac{-6}{81}=-\frac{2}{27}$

↑ problem
10.

Same pattern with $(x-1)$ as the squared quantity.

$\displaystyle\frac{1}{(x-1)^2}-\frac{1}{16}=\frac{16-(x-1)^2}{16(x-1)^2}=\frac{-\big[(x-1)-4\big]\big[(x-1)+4\big]}{16(x-1)^2}=\frac{-(x-5)(x+3)}{16(x-1)^2}$

Dividing by $(x-5)$ cancels:

$\displaystyle\frac{-(x-5)(x+3)}{16(x-1)^2(x-5)}=\frac{-(x+3)}{16(x-1)^2}$

$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 5}\frac{\frac{1}{(x-1)^2}-\frac{1}{16}}{x-5}=\frac{-(5+3)}{16(4)^2}=\frac{-8}{256}=-\frac{1}{32}$

↑ problem

Conjugates — Square Root Minus a Constant

11.

Multiply by the conjugate $\sqrt{x+3}+2$ over itself.

$\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{x+3}-2}{x-1}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{x+3}+2}{\sqrt{x+3}+2}=\frac{(x+3)-4}{(x-1)(\sqrt{x+3}+2)}=\frac{x-1}{(x-1)(\sqrt{x+3}+2)}$

Cancel $(x-1)$:

$\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{x+3}+2}$

$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 1}\frac{\sqrt{x+3}-2}{x-1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{4}+2}=\frac{1}{4}$

↑ problem
12.

Multiply by the conjugate $\sqrt{x+5}+3$.

$\displaystyle\frac{(x+5)-9}{(x-4)(\sqrt{x+5}+3)}=\frac{x-4}{(x-4)(\sqrt{x+5}+3)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{x+5}+3}$

$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 4}\frac{\sqrt{x+5}-3}{x-4}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{9}+3}=\frac{1}{6}$

↑ problem
13.

Multiply by the conjugate $\sqrt{x+9}+4$.

$\displaystyle\frac{(x+9)-16}{(x-7)(\sqrt{x+9}+4)}=\frac{x-7}{(x-7)(\sqrt{x+9}+4)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{x+9}+4}$

$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 7}\frac{\sqrt{x+9}-4}{x-7}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{16}+4}=\frac{1}{8}$

↑ problem
14.

Multiply by the conjugate $\sqrt{x+9}+5$.

$\displaystyle\frac{(x+9)-25}{(x-16)(\sqrt{x+9}+5)}=\frac{x-16}{(x-16)(\sqrt{x+9}+5)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{x+9}+5}$

$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 16}\frac{\sqrt{x+9}-5}{x-16}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{25}+5}=\frac{1}{10}$

↑ problem
15.

The radical is on the bottom this time — multiply top and bottom by $\sqrt{x+5}+2$.

$\displaystyle\frac{x+1}{\sqrt{x+5}-2}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{x+5}+2}{\sqrt{x+5}+2}=\frac{(x+1)\big(\sqrt{x+5}+2\big)}{(x+5)-4}=\frac{(x+1)\big(\sqrt{x+5}+2\big)}{x+1}$

Cancel $(x+1)$:

$\displaystyle=\sqrt{x+5}+2$

$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to -1}\frac{x+1}{\sqrt{x+5}-2}=\sqrt{4}+2=4$

↑ problem
16.

Multiply top and bottom by $\sqrt{x+7}+3$.

$\displaystyle\frac{(x-2)\big(\sqrt{x+7}+3\big)}{(x+7)-9}=\frac{(x-2)\big(\sqrt{x+7}+3\big)}{x-2}=\sqrt{x+7}+3$

$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 2}\frac{x-2}{\sqrt{x+7}-3}=\sqrt{9}+3=6$

↑ problem
17.

Multiply top and bottom by $\sqrt{x+6}+4$.

$\displaystyle\frac{(x-10)\big(\sqrt{x+6}+4\big)}{(x+6)-16}=\frac{(x-10)\big(\sqrt{x+6}+4\big)}{x-10}=\sqrt{x+6}+4$

$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 10}\frac{x-10}{\sqrt{x+6}-4}=\sqrt{16}+4=8$

↑ problem

Conjugates — Coefficient Inside the Root

18.

Multiply by the conjugate $\sqrt{2x+9}+3$. The coefficient $2$ survives into the numerator.

$\displaystyle\frac{(2x+9)-9}{x\big(\sqrt{2x+9}+3\big)}=\frac{2x}{x\big(\sqrt{2x+9}+3\big)}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{2x+9}+3}$

$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{\sqrt{2x+9}-3}{x}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{9}+3}=\frac{2}{6}=\frac{1}{3}$

↑ problem
19.

Multiply by the conjugate $\sqrt{3x+16}+4$.

$\displaystyle\frac{(3x+16)-16}{x\big(\sqrt{3x+16}+4\big)}=\frac{3x}{x\big(\sqrt{3x+16}+4\big)}=\frac{3}{\sqrt{3x+16}+4}$

$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{\sqrt{3x+16}-4}{x}=\frac{3}{\sqrt{16}+4}=\frac{3}{8}$

↑ problem
20.

Multiply by the conjugate $\sqrt{2x+12}+4$.

$\displaystyle\frac{(2x+12)-16}{(x-2)\big(\sqrt{2x+12}+4\big)}=\frac{2x-4}{(x-2)\big(\sqrt{2x+12}+4\big)}=\frac{2(x-2)}{(x-2)\big(\sqrt{2x+12}+4\big)}$

Cancel $(x-2)$:

$\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\sqrt{2x+12}+4}$

$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 2}\frac{\sqrt{2x+12}-4}{x-2}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{16}+4}=\frac{2}{8}=\frac{1}{4}$

↑ problem
21.

Multiply by the conjugate $\sqrt{3x+10}+5$.

$\displaystyle\frac{(3x+10)-25}{(x-5)\big(\sqrt{3x+10}+5\big)}=\frac{3x-15}{(x-5)\big(\sqrt{3x+10}+5\big)}=\frac{3(x-5)}{(x-5)\big(\sqrt{3x+10}+5\big)}$

Cancel $(x-5)$:

$\displaystyle=\frac{3}{\sqrt{3x+10}+5}$

$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 5}\frac{\sqrt{3x+10}-5}{x-5}=\frac{3}{\sqrt{25}+5}=\frac{3}{10}$

↑ problem
22.

The radical is on the bottom — multiply top and bottom by $\sqrt{2x+3}+3$.

$\displaystyle\frac{(x-3)\big(\sqrt{2x+3}+3\big)}{(2x+3)-9}=\frac{(x-3)\big(\sqrt{2x+3}+3\big)}{2x-6}=\frac{(x-3)\big(\sqrt{2x+3}+3\big)}{2(x-3)}$

Cancel $(x-3)$:

$\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{2x+3}+3}{2}$

$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 3}\frac{x-3}{\sqrt{2x+3}-3}=\frac{\sqrt{9}+3}{2}=\frac{6}{2}=3$
