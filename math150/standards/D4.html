D4: Basic Derivative Rules & Tangent Lines

22 practice problems on differentiating elementary functions with the power rule and using derivatives to find tangent lines.

  • The power rule $\frac{d}{dx}\left[ax^n\right]=anx^{n-1}$ works for any exponent $n$ — negative, fractional, whatever. The hard part is usually rewriting the function into $ax^n$ form first.
  • Reciprocal powers rewrite with a negative exponent: $\dfrac{1}{x^k}=x^{-k}$, and any constant in the denominator comes along too: $\dfrac{1}{cx^k}=\dfrac{1}{c}x^{-k}$.
  • Roots rewrite with a fractional exponent: $\sqrt{x}=x^{1/2}$, $\sqrt[3]{x}=x^{1/3}$, and a root of a power combines the exponents, e.g. $\sqrt{x^3}=x^{3/2}$.
  • Differentiate a sum term-by-term — each term keeps its own coefficient and exponent through the power rule; a lone constant term always differentiates to $0$.
  • To find a tangent line at $x=a$: find the point by computing $f(a)$, find the slope by computing $f'(a)$, then write $y-f(a)=f'(a)(x-a)$.

Rewriting Before Differentiating

1.

$f(x)=\dfrac{1}{2x^3}=\dfrac{1}{2}x^{-3}$

$f'(x)=\dfrac{1}{2}(-3)x^{-4}=-\dfrac{3}{2}x^{-4}=-\dfrac{3}{2x^4}$

↑ problem
2.

$f(x)=5\sqrt{x}=5x^{1/2}$

$f'(x)=5\cdot\dfrac{1}{2}x^{-1/2}=\dfrac{5}{2}x^{-1/2}=\dfrac{5}{2\sqrt{x}}$

↑ problem
3.

$f(x)=\dfrac{1}{4x^2}=\dfrac{1}{4}x^{-2}$

$f'(x)=\dfrac{1}{4}(-2)x^{-3}=-\dfrac{1}{2}x^{-3}=-\dfrac{1}{2x^3}$

↑ problem
