D7: The Chain Rule

24 practice problems on the Chain Rule, building from a single "outer" wrapper around a simple "inner" function up to two or three layers stacked together.

  • Chain Rule: for $y=f(g(x))$, $\;y'=f'(g(x))\cdot g'(x)$ — derivative of the outside (leaving the inside alone), times derivative of the inside.
  • Identify the outer function first — the last operation that would happen if you evaluated the expression by hand (a power, a trig function, an exponential, a log) — then everything inside it is the "inner" function you multiply by the derivative of.
  • A power on the outside stays a power rule step: $\dfrac{d}{dx}\left[g(x)\right]^n=n\left[g(x)\right]^{n-1}\cdot g'(x)$ — the exponent drops down, but the base stays untouched.
  • With two or more layers, peel off one outer function at a time and keep multiplying by the derivative of whatever's left inside — don't try to do it all in your head at once.
  • A function like $\sin^2(x)$ is shorthand for $(\sin x)^2$ — the outer layer is the squaring, and $\sin x$ is the inner function.

Power of a Function

1.

Outer: $(\;)^4$. Inner: $3x+1$, with derivative $3$.

$y'=4(3x+1)^3\cdot 3=12(3x+1)^3$

↑ problem
2.

Outer: $(\;)^3$. Inner: $x^2-5$, with derivative $2x$.

$y'=3(x^2-5)^2\cdot 2x=6x(x^2-5)^2$

↑ problem
3.

$y=(x^2+1)^{1/2}$. Outer: $(\;)^{1/2}$. Inner: $x^2+1$, with derivative $2x$.

$y'=\dfrac{1}{2}(x^2+1)^{-1/2}\cdot 2x=\dfrac{x}{\sqrt{x^2+1}}$

↑ problem
4.

$y'=-2(2x+3)^{-3}\cdot 2=-\dfrac{4}{(2x+3)^3}$

5.

$y'=5(4-x^3)^4\cdot(-3x^2)=-15x^2(4-x^3)^4$

$y'=\dfrac{1}{3}(5x-2)^{-2/3}\cdot 5=\dfrac{5}{3(5x-2)^{2/3}}$
