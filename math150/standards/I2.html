I2: Antiderivatives Needing Simplification or Geometry

16 practice problems: rational integrands that need to be split apart algebraically before you can integrate, and definite integrals of $\sqrt{r^2-x^2}$ that are much faster by geometry than by any antiderivative formula.

  • Splitting a fraction: $\dfrac{a+b+c}{d}=\dfrac{a}{d}+\dfrac{b}{d}+\dfrac{c}{d}$ — break a single fraction with a sum on top into several simpler fractions, each with the same denominator, before trying to integrate.
  • Once split, each piece is a power of $x$ (possibly negative or fractional) — from there it's the same power rule as I1.
  • Geometric interpretation: $y=\sqrt{r^2-x^2}$ is the top half of the circle $x^2+y^2=r^2$. A definite integral of it over some interval is the area of a circular region — no antiderivative formula needed, just $\text{Area}=\frac12\pi r^2\cdot(\text{fraction of the circle})$.
  • A minus sign in front of the square root reflects the curve below the $x$-axis — the region is congruent, but the signed area (the value of the integral) is negative.

Solutions

Algebraic Simplification

1.

$\displaystyle\frac{4x^3-2x+5}{x^2}=4x-\frac{2}{x}+\frac{5}{x^2}$

$\displaystyle\int\left(4x-\frac2x+5x^{-2}\right)dx=2x^2-2\ln|x|-\frac5x+C$

↑ problem
2.

$\displaystyle\frac{2x^4+3x^2-6}{x^3}=2x+\frac{3}{x}-\frac{6}{x^3}$

$\displaystyle\int\left(2x+\frac3x-6x^{-3}\right)dx=x^2+3\ln|x|+\frac{3}{x^2}+C$

↑ problem
3.

$\displaystyle\frac{x^5-4x^3+2x}{x^4}=x-\frac{4}{x}+\frac{2}{x^3}$

$\displaystyle\int\left(x-\frac4x+2x^{-3}\right)dx=\frac{x^2}{2}-4\ln|x|-\frac{1}{x^2}+C$

↑ problem
4.

$\displaystyle\frac{6x^2-3x+2}{x^4}=\frac{6}{x^2}-\frac{3}{x^3}+\frac{2}{x^4}$

$\displaystyle\int\left(6x^{-2}-3x^{-3}+2x^{-4}\right)dx=-\frac6x+\frac{3}{2x^2}-\frac{2}{3x^3}+C$

↑ problem
5.

$\displaystyle\frac{5x^6-x^3+4}{x^5}=5x-\frac{1}{x^2}+\frac{4}{x^5}$

$\displaystyle\int\left(5x-x^{-2}+4x^{-5}\right)dx=\frac{5}{2}x^2+\frac1x-\frac{1}{x^4}+C$

↑ problem
6.

$\displaystyle\frac{3x^3+2x^2-x}{x^2}=3x+2-\frac{1}{x}$

$\displaystyle\int\left(3x+2-\frac1x\right)dx=\frac32x^2+2x-\ln|x|+C$

↑ problem
7.

$\displaystyle\frac{x^4+3x^2-2}{x^3}=x+\frac{3}{x}-\frac{2}{x^3}$

$\displaystyle\int\left(x+\frac3x-2x^{-3}\right)dx=\frac{x^2}{2}+3\ln|x|+\frac{1}{x^2}+C$

↑ problem
8.

$\displaystyle\frac{x+1}{\sqrt{x}}=\frac{x}{\sqrt{x}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{x}}=x^{1/2}+x^{-1/2}$

$\displaystyle\int\left(x^{1/2}+x^{-1/2}\right)dx=\frac23x^{3/2}+2x^{1/2}+C$

↑ problem

Geometric Interpretation — Circular Area

9.

$y=\sqrt{9-x^2}$ is the top half of the circle $x^2+y^2=9$ ($r=3$). Integrating from $0$ to $3$ sweeps out exactly one quarter of the circle.

$\displaystyle\int_{0}^{3}\sqrt{9-x^2}\,dx=\frac14\pi(3)^2=\frac{9\pi}{4}$

↑ problem
10.

Same circle, $r=3$, but the quarter on the other side of the $y$-axis — same area by symmetry.

$\displaystyle\int_{-3}^{0}\sqrt{9-x^2}\,dx=\frac14\pi(3)^2=\frac{9\pi}{4}$

↑ problem
11.

The minus sign flips the curve below the $x$-axis. Same quarter-circle region, but the signed area is negative.

$\displaystyle\int_{0}^{3}-\sqrt{9-x^2}\,dx=-\frac{9\pi}{4}$

↑ problem
12.

$r=4$, one quarter of the circle.

$\displaystyle\int_{-4}^{0}\sqrt{16-x^2}\,dx=\frac14\pi(4)^2=4\pi$

↑ problem
13.

$r=5$, one quarter of the circle, reflected below the axis by the minus sign.

$\displaystyle\int_{0}^{5}-\sqrt{25-x^2}\,dx=-\frac14\pi(5)^2=-\frac{25\pi}{4}$

↑ problem
14.

The bounds span the full diameter $-5$ to $5$, so this sweeps out the entire upper half of the circle — a semicircle.

$\displaystyle\int_{-5}^{5}\sqrt{25-x^2}\,dx=\frac12\pi(5)^2=\frac{25\pi}{2}$

↑ problem
15.

$r=6$, one quarter of the circle, reflected below the axis.

$\displaystyle\int_{-6}^{0}-\sqrt{36-x^2}\,dx=-\frac14\pi(6)^2=-9\pi$

↑ problem
16.

Full diameter $-2$ to $2$: a semicircle of radius $2$.

$\displaystyle\int_{-2}^{2}\sqrt{4-x^2}\,dx=\frac12\pi(2)^2=2\pi$
