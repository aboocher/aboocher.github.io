D6: Product and Quotient Rules

20 practice problems on the Product and Quotient Rules. Every function here is built from pieces you already know how to differentiate (power, trig, exponential, log, inverse trig) — you don't need to simplify the final answer.

  • Product Rule: for $y=u(x)\cdot v(x)$, $\;y'=u'v+uv'$ — derivative of the first times the second, plus the first times derivative of the second.
  • Quotient Rule: for $y=\dfrac{u(x)}{v(x)}$, $\;y'=\dfrac{u'v-uv'}{v^2}$ — order matters here (unlike the product rule), and the denominator gets squared.
  • Identify $u$ and $v$ first, write down $u'$ and $v'$ separately, then assemble the formula — trying to do it all in one step is where sign errors creep in.
  • Quotient rule numerators often simplify a lot after expanding — don't panic if the answer collapses to something short like a constant over a square.

Product Rule

1.

$u=x^3,\;v=\sin x \;\Rightarrow\; u'=3x^2,\;v'=\cos x$

$y'=3x^2\sin x+x^3\cos x$

↑ problem
2.

$u=x^2,\;v=e^x \;\Rightarrow\; u'=2x,\;v'=e^x$

$y'=2xe^x+x^2e^x$

3.

$u=\sqrt{x}=x^{1/2},\;v=\cos x \;\Rightarrow\; u'=\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{x}},\;v'=-\sin x$

$y'=\dfrac{\cos x}{2\sqrt{x}}-\sqrt{x}\sin x$

↑ problem
4.

$u=x^4,\;v=\ln x \;\Rightarrow\; u'=4x^3,\;v'=\dfrac{1}{x}$

$y'=4x^3\ln x+x^4\cdot\dfrac{1}{x}=4x^3\ln x+x^3$

↑ problem
5.

$y'=\dfrac{\arctan x}{x}+\dfrac{\ln x}{1+x^2}$

Quotient Rule

11.

$u=2x+1,\;v=x^2+3 \;\Rightarrow\; u'=2,\;v'=2x$

$f'(x)=\dfrac{2(x^2+3)-(2x+1)(2x)}{(x^2+3)^2}=\dfrac{2x^2+6-4x^2-2x}{(x^2+3)^2}=\dfrac{-2x^2-2x+6}{(x^2+3)^2}$

12.

$u=\sin x,\;v=x \;\Rightarrow\; u'=\cos x,\;v'=1$

$f'(x)=\dfrac{x\cos x-\sin x}{x^2}$

↑ problem
13.

$u=x^2,\;v=e^x \;\Rightarrow\; u'=2x,\;v'=e^x$

$f'(x)=\dfrac{2xe^x-x^2e^x}{(e^x)^2}=\dfrac{e^x(2x-x^2)}{e^{2x}}=\dfrac{2x-x^2}{e^x}$

14.

$u=\ln x,\;v=x^3 \;\Rightarrow\; u'=\dfrac{1}{x},\;v'=3x^2$

$f'(x)=\dfrac{\frac{1}{x}x^3-\ln x\cdot 3x^2}{x^6}=\dfrac{x^2-3x^2\ln x}{x^6}=\dfrac{1-3\ln x}{x^4}$

$u=e^x,\;v=x^2+1 \;\Rightarrow\; u'=e^x,\;v'=2x$

$f'(x)=\dfrac{e^x(x^2+1)-e^x(2x)}{(x^2+1)^2}=\dfrac{e^x(x^2-2x+1)}{(x^2+1)^2}=\dfrac{e^x(x-1)^2}{(x^2+1)^2}$
