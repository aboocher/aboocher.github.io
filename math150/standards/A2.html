A2: Implicit Differentiation & Tangent Lines

16 practice problems: 10 on finding $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ implicitly, then 6 full curve-and-tangent-line problems that put the whole workflow together.

  • Differentiate both sides of the equation with respect to $x$, treating $y$ as an unknown function of $x$. Every time you differentiate a $y$-term, the chain rule tacks on a $y'$ (often written $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$).
  • A term like $xy$ needs the product rule: $\dfrac{d}{dx}[xy]=y+xy'$.
  • After differentiating, every term is either "known" or has a $y'$ attached. Collect all the $y'$ terms on one side, factor $y'$ out, and divide to solve for $y'$.
  • For a full tangent-line problem: first pin down the actual point $(x_0,y_0)$ on the curve (either solve for it or verify a given one), then find $y'$ as a formula, then substitute the point into that formula to get a single numerical slope, then write $y-y_0=y'(x-x_0)$.

Solutions

Finding dy/dx Implicitly

1.

$2x+2yy'=0$

$y'=-\dfrac{x}{y}$

↑ problem
2.

The $xy$ term needs the product rule: $\dfrac{d}{dx}[xy]=y+xy'$.

$2x+y+xy'+2yy'=0$

$y'(x+2y)=-2x-y$

$y'=-\dfrac{2x+y}{x+2y}$

↑ problem
3.

$3x^2-3y^2y'=6y+6xy'$

$3x^2-6y=3y^2y'+6xy'$

$y'(3y^2+6x)=3x^2-6y$

$y'=\dfrac{3x^2-6y}{3y^2+6x}=\dfrac{x^2-2y}{y^2+2x}$

↑ problem
4.

The left side needs the chain rule outside and the product rule inside: $\dfrac{d}{dx}\sin(xy)=\cos(xy)\cdot(y+xy')$.

$\cos(xy)(y+xy')=1+y'$

$y\cos(xy)+x\cos(xy)y'=1+y'$

$y'\big[x\cos(xy)-1\big]=1-y\cos(xy)$

$y'=\dfrac{1-y\cos(xy)}{x\cos(xy)-1}$

↑ problem
5.

$\dfrac{d}{dx}e^{xy}=e^{xy}(y+xy')$.

$e^{xy}(y+xy')=1-y'$

$ye^{xy}+xe^{xy}y'=1-y'$

$y'\big[xe^{xy}+1\big]=1-ye^{xy}$

$y'=\dfrac{1-ye^{xy}}{xe^{xy}+1}$

↑ problem
6.

$2xy+x^2y'+3y^2y'=1$

$y'(x^2+3y^2)=1-2xy$

$y'=\dfrac{1-2xy}{x^2+3y^2}$

↑ problem
7.

$\dfrac{1}{y}y'+y+xy'=0$

$y'\left(\dfrac{1}{y}+x\right)=-y$

Multiply top and bottom by $y$ to clear the small fraction:

$y'=\dfrac{-y}{\frac{1}{y}+x}=\dfrac{-y^2}{1+xy}$

↑ problem
8.

$-\sin x+2yy'=\cos(y)y'$

$y'(2y-\cos y)=\sin x$

$y'=\dfrac{\sin x}{2y-\cos y}$

↑ problem
9.

$2xy^3+x^2\cdot 3y^2y'=0$

$3x^2y^2y'=-2xy^3$

$y'=\dfrac{-2xy^3}{3x^2y^2}=-\dfrac{2y}{3x}$

↑ problem
10.

$\sec^2(y)y'=2xy+x^2y'$

$y'\big[\sec^2 y-x^2\big]=2xy$

$y'=\dfrac{2xy}{\sec^2 y-x^2}$

↑ problem

Full Workflow — Point and Tangent Line

11.

Find the point: substitute $x=2$: $4+2y+y^2=12\Rightarrow y^2+2y-8=0\Rightarrow(y+4)(y-2)=0$, so $y=2$ or $y=-4$. Since $y>0$, $P=(2,2)$.

Differentiate implicitly: $2x+y+xy'+2yy'=0\Rightarrow y'(x+2y)=-2x-y$

Evaluate at $P$: $y'=\dfrac{-4-2}{2+4}=\dfrac{-6}{6}=-1$

Tangent line: $y-2=-1(x-2)$

↑ problem
12.

Verify: $3^3+3^3=27+27=54$ and $6(3)(3)=54$ — equal, so $P(3,3)$ is on the curve.

Differentiate implicitly: $3x^2-3y^2y'=6y+6xy'\Rightarrow y'(3y^2-6x)=6y-3x^2$

Evaluate at $P$: $y'=\dfrac{18-27}{27-18}=\dfrac{-9}{9}=-1$

Tangent line: $y-3=-1(x-3)$

↑ problem
13.

Find the point: substitute $x=1$: $1-y+y^2=3\Rightarrow y^2-y-2=0\Rightarrow(y-2)(y+1)=0$, so $y=2$ or $y=-1$. Taking the larger value, $P=(1,2)$.

Differentiate implicitly: $2x-y-xy'+2yy'=0\Rightarrow y'(2y-x)=y-2x$

Evaluate at $P$: $y'=\dfrac{2-2}{4-1}=\dfrac{0}{3}=0$ — a horizontal tangent.

Tangent line: $y-2=0(x-1)$, i.e. $y=2$

↑ problem
14.

Find the point: substitute $y=\pi$: $x\cos(\pi)+\pi^2=\pi^2\Rightarrow -x=0\Rightarrow x=0$. So $P=(0,\pi)$.

Differentiate implicitly (product rule on $x\cos y$): $\cos y-x\sin(y)y'+2yy'=0\Rightarrow y'(2y-x\sin y)=-\cos y$

Evaluate at $P$: $y'=\dfrac{-\cos\pi}{2\pi-0}=\dfrac{1}{2\pi}$

Tangent line: $y-\pi=\dfrac{1}{2\pi}(x-0)$

↑ problem
15.

Verify: $2^3+4^3=8+64=72$ and $9(2)(4)=72$ — equal, so $P(2,4)$ is on the curve.

Differentiate implicitly: $3x^2+3y^2y'=9y+9xy'\Rightarrow y'(3y^2-9x)=9y-3x^2$

Evaluate at $P$: $y'=\dfrac{36-12}{48-18}=\dfrac{24}{30}=\dfrac{4}{5}$

Tangent line: $y-4=\dfrac{4}{5}(x-2)$

↑ problem
16.

Verify: $\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)+\cos\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)=1+0=1$ — true, so $P\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2}\right)$ is on the curve.

Differentiate implicitly: $\cos x-\sin(y)y'=0\Rightarrow y'=\dfrac{\cos x}{\sin y}$

Evaluate at $P$: $y'=\dfrac{\cos(\pi/2)}{\sin(\pi/2)}=\dfrac{0}{1}=0$ — a horizontal tangent.

Tangent line: $y-\dfrac{\pi}{2}=0\left(x-\dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)$, i.e. $y=\dfrac{\pi}{2}$
