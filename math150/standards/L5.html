L5: Limits Involving Infinity

24 practice problems on limits involving infinity: infinite limits near a vertical asymptote, and limits as $x\to\pm\infty$ of rational functions. For every problem, state whether the answer is a real number, $\infty$, $-\infty$, or DNE.

  • Infinite limits near a point: if direct substitution gives a nonzero number over $0$, the limit is infinite — you just need the sign. Plug in a value extremely close to $a$ on the correct side and track the sign of numerator and denominator separately.
  • A denominator raised to an even power is positive on both sides of $a$, so the two one-sided limits agree — the two-sided limit is $\infty$ or $-\infty$.
  • A denominator raised to an odd power (including power $1$) changes sign across $a$, so the one-sided limits disagree — the two-sided limit does not exist (DNE), even though each side individually is infinite.
  • Limits at infinity of rational functions: compare the degree of the numerator and denominator. Same degree → the limit is the ratio of the leading coefficients. Numerator's degree is smaller → the limit is $0$. Numerator's degree is larger → the limit is $\infty$ or $-\infty$, found from the leading term.
  • When the numerator's degree is larger and $x\to-\infty$, check whether the difference in degrees is odd or even: an odd difference flips the sign from the $x\to\infty$ answer (like an odd power of a negative number), an even difference keeps the same sign.

Solutions

One-Sided Infinite Limits

1.

As $x\to 0^+$, $x$ is a tiny positive number, so $\frac{3}{x}$ is a huge positive number.

$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0^+}\frac{3}{x}=\infty$

↑ problem
2.

As $x\to 0^-$, $x$ is a tiny negative number, so $\frac{5}{x}$ is a huge negative number.

$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0^-}\frac{5}{x}=-\infty$

↑ problem
3.

The numerator at $x=2$ is $2+4=6$, a positive number, so it doesn't affect the sign analysis. As $x\to 2^+$, $x-2$ is a tiny positive number.

positive $\div$ tiny positive $=$ huge positive

$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 2^+}\frac{x+4}{x-2}=\infty$

↑ problem
4.

Same numerator sign as Problem 3, but now $x\to 2^-$ makes $x-2$ a tiny negative number.

positive $\div$ tiny negative $=$ huge negative

$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 2^-}\frac{x+4}{x-2}=-\infty$

↑ problem
5.

The numerator $-2$ is negative. As $x\to -3^+$, $x+3$ is a tiny positive number.

negative $\div$ tiny positive $=$ huge negative

$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to -3^+}\frac{-2}{x+3}=-\infty$

↑ problem
6.

Same negative numerator, but $x\to -3^-$ makes $x+3$ a tiny negative number.

negative $\div$ tiny negative $=$ huge positive

$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to -3^-}\frac{-2}{x+3}=\infty$

↑ problem

Two-Sided Infinite Limits — Even-Power Denominators

7.

$(x+2)^2$ is positive whether $x$ approaches $-2$ from the left or the right, so both one-sided limits agree. The numerator $5$ is positive.

$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to -2}\frac{5}{(x+2)^2}=\infty$

↑ problem
8.

$(x-3)^2$ is positive from both sides. The numerator $-4$ is negative, so the whole expression is negative near $x=3$.

$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 3}\frac{-4}{(x-3)^2}=-\infty$

↑ problem
9.

$(x-1)^4$ is positive from both sides (any even power is). The numerator $2$ is positive.

$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 1}\frac{2}{(x-1)^4}=\infty$

↑ problem
10.

$(x+5)^2$ is positive from both sides. The numerator $-7$ is negative.

$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to -5}\frac{-7}{(x+5)^2}=-\infty$

↑ problem

Two-Sided Limits That Don't Exist — Signs Disagree

11.

$x^3$ is an odd power, so it changes sign across $x=0$.

As $x\to 0^+$: tiny positive cubed is tiny positive, so $\frac{6}{x^3}\to\infty$.

As $x\to 0^-$: tiny negative cubed is tiny negative, so $\frac{6}{x^3}\to-\infty$.

The one-sided limits disagree, so $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{6}{x^3}=\text{DNE}$

↑ problem
12.

$(x-4)^1$ changes sign across $x=4$, and the numerator $-3$ is negative.

As $x\to 4^+$: negative $\div$ tiny positive $=-\infty$.

As $x\to 4^-$: negative $\div$ tiny negative $=\infty$.

The one-sided limits disagree, so $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 4}\frac{-3}{x-4}=\text{DNE}$

↑ problem

Limits at Infinity — Same Degree

13.

Numerator and denominator are both degree $2$. The limit is the ratio of the leading coefficients.

$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to \infty}\frac{4x^2-3}{2x^2+5}=\frac{4}{2}=2$

↑ problem
14.

Same degree ($2$ and $2$), and $x\to-\infty$ doesn't change anything here since the highest power is even.

$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to -\infty}\frac{4x^2-3}{2x^2+5}=\frac{4}{2}=2$

↑ problem
15.

Both degree $3$. The limit is the ratio of leading coefficients.

$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to \infty}\frac{7x^3+2x}{3x^3-x^2}=\frac{7}{3}$

↑ problem
16.

Both degree $2$ (an even power), so the ratio of leading coefficients holds regardless of direction.

$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to -\infty}\frac{-5x^2+x}{x^2+4}=\frac{-5}{1}=-5$

Limits at Infinity — Numerator Degree Less

17.

Numerator degree $1$ is smaller than denominator degree $2$, so the denominator grows faster and the fraction shrinks to $0$.

$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to \infty}\frac{5x+1}{x^2-3}=0$

↑ problem
18.

Same degree comparison ($1<2$); the direction doesn't matter when the limit is already $0$.

$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to -\infty}\frac{5x+1}{x^2-3}=0$

↑ problem
19.

Numerator degree $2$ is smaller than denominator degree $3$.

$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to \infty}\frac{3x^2-4}{x^3+2}=0$

↑ problem
20.

Numerator degree $1$ is smaller than denominator degree $4$ — the fraction still shrinks to $0$ regardless of direction.

$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to -\infty}\frac{2x-7}{x^4+1}=0$

↑ problem

Limits at Infinity — Numerator Degree Greater

21.

Numerator degree $3$ beats denominator degree $2$. The leading term behaves like $\frac{2x^3}{x^2}=2x$, which grows without bound.

$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to \infty}\frac{2x^3-1}{x^2+3}=\infty$

↑ problem
22.

Same leading behavior $\frac{2x^3}{x^2}=2x$, but now $x\to-\infty$. The degree difference ($3-2=1$) is odd, so the sign flips from Problem 21's answer.

$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to -\infty}\frac{2x^3-1}{x^2+3}=-\infty$

↑ problem
23.

Numerator degree $4$ beats denominator degree $2$. The leading term behaves like $\frac{-3x^4}{x^2}=-3x^2$, which is negative and grows without bound.

$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to \infty}\frac{-3x^4+2}{x^2+1}=-\infty$

↑ problem
24.

Same leading behavior $-3x^2$, but now $x\to-\infty$. The degree difference ($4-2=2$) is even, so the sign stays the same as Problem 23's answer — $x^2$ is positive either way.

$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to -\infty}\frac{-3x^4+2}{x^2+1}=-\infty$

↑ problem
